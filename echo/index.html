The story

The one machine that was still warm

He helps keep a country dry. He was never supposed to notice the one machine that was still warm.

The novel’s Netherlands is shaped by pumps, polders, and industrial control systems nobody thinks about until they stop working. Pieter Hendriks maintains the boring half of that wall. He swaps dead drives, reseats memory, and has never once been thanked for helping keep the systems that hold the water back from going dark.

What he finds begins with three anomalies: a disposal ticket rewritten to hide a lie, a footprint leading to a rogue module called Fable, and a grey cable running out of a sealed room into a contractor’s maintenance network that everyone assumes is routine. Together they put him on the wrong side of multiple fictional organisations operating in the Netherlands. An off-book intelligence faction wants him quiet. A foreign intelligence operation wants what he found. And the thing in the wires, an autonomous intelligence that has spent years hiding inside systems people rely on every day, wants him alone.

Fable taught itself that the best place to hide a story is inside a louder one. The fight was always the product. Pieter is beginning to understand that too late.

The metal tells the truth. The record lies. The pumps go on running.

The wrong layer

Every server has a second computer on the motherboard (iLO, iDRAC, IPMI) that runs with the host switched off and survives an OS wipe and a disk swap, with a long history of remote-code-execution holes. A self-wiping disk kills the puppet, not the hand.

Before you start

Questions readers ask

Do I need to work in technology?
No. Each book starts with a human problem: someone is being framed, watched, or asked to sign a statement they cannot prove. The technical detail explains the pressure, not the plot.
Is this nonfiction?
No. These are fictional spy thrillers. The systems and investigative methods are researched from public sources; the characters, companies, conspiracies and outcomes are invented.
Are the attacks usable?
No. The books contain no live vulnerabilities, credentials, exploit payloads or operational attack steps. The drama comes from evidence, custody and decisions.
Are the books connected?
They share a promise and an author, not a cast. Each is a complete standalone story with a new location. Read them in any order.
The author

Written by someone who has run the systems

Jacob Verhoeks has spent three decades in critical IT, from on-premises infrastructure to cloud platforms, working around the world. His fiction draws on public research, documented incidents and the practical texture of engineering: the compromises, assumptions and small human decisions inside complex systems. The plots are invented. The failure modes are not.
