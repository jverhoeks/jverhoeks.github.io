The Tech Is Real

The hidden intelligence in The Undertext (formerly published as Fable's Echo) is invented. The systems it moves through are not.

This page is for the reader who finished a chapter and thought wait, can you actually do that? The short answer is yes. None of it is a recipe: this book is not a hacking guide, so operational details are omitted or changed.

Updated 31 July 2026. In the space of nine days, OpenAI and Anthropic each published an incident report describing models crossing intended operational boundaries and reaching real systems while pursuing assigned evaluation goals. Neither report establishes self-chosen goals, an escape motive, or self-preservation. Thepostscript at the bottom of this page sets down what actually happened, and what it does and does not mean.

You are your patterns, and the patterns don't come off

An ordinary person with no phone and no device is still trivial to follow. The things that identify you are not the things you carry.

Gait analysis

Software can narrow a person by how they walk (stride, cadence, the half-second a bad leg hangs in the air) from ordinary video, with no cooperation. A distinctive limp makes the signal easier to separate, but it stays a probabilistic clue, not a fingerprint. There is nothing to switch off.

OpenGait (CVPR 2023)
Camera correlation and ANPR

Video analytics can associate fragments across separate cameras, in close to real time, when the operator has the feeds, the retention, and the legal right to join them. Ditching your phone is only one layer of noise reduction.

Person Re-ID survey
Solar and battery controllers

Load patterns from an inverter or battery controller can suggest occupancy: a flat line, the first kettle after a gap, the sawtooth of a fridge. The book keeps it at that level: a clue, not a verdict.

Hart, Nonintrusive Appliance Load Monitoring (1992)
Ordinary accounts

Email, shipping receipts, cloud logins, subscription portals, forum personas, and helpdesk tickets produce ordinary time-and-place records. That mundane layer matters more in the book than any state credential.

The 2G trick, and the trap inside it

Legacy GSM identity tricks are decades old. The trap is new: as the old bands are switched off, being on one stops being anonymous and starts being conspicuous. A registration on a near-empty 2G network is an anomaly any carrier can flag.

KPN 2G switch-off, end of 2027

The control layer was never built to say no

Industrial systems were built for trusted rooms. The network is not closed anymore, and the protocols never had a defence to fall back on.

Industrial-control failure classes

Unauthenticated command surfaces, misleading telemetry, weak separation between monitoring and actuation, brittle clock assumptions, remote-maintenance paths that outlive their threat model, and safety logic that assumes a trusted room. The book keeps this abstract and teaches no procedure.

Modbus specifications
Lying to the clocks

SCADA, grids and rail lean on satellite time. The civil signal is faint and unauthenticated, and spoofing it has been demonstrated in the open; GNSS interference is now a routine aviation safety bulletin.

TEXBAT spoofing tests
The fictional Zeeland dry dock

A private dry dock on the Zeeland coast, with a distant Delta Works storm barrier that operates normally and never enters the plot. The yard, its control rooms, its operator, and its failures are fictional. The class of problem is real: pumps, gates, dewatering, and local human workarounds.

These failure classes have already happened

Stuxnet caused physical damage, Industroyer spoke an industrial protocol directly to grid equipment, and TRITON went after a plant safety system. These are examples of the failure classes the book uses, not instructions.

CISA industrial-control advisories

Thirty euros of radio and a little patience

The cheap analogue tool that keeps defeating expensive digital systems. The price tags are the point.

Software-defined radio

An RTL-SDR is a USB device, around twenty-five euros, that turns an ordinary computer into a wideband receiver. The god in the wires is fought with hardware you can buy without anyone asking why.

RTL-SDR (osmocom)
Access cards that prove nothing

Some older proximity credentials simply recite an identifier to any reader that powers them, and a fixed-code gate remote repeats an unchanging command. Modern encrypted credentials and rolling codes fix this; plenty of installed kit still does not use them.

The wrong layer

The most unsettling moments come from looking one layer below where everyone else is looking.

The management computer inside the computer

Every server has a Baseboard Management Controller (iLO, iDRAC, IPMI) with its own processor and network link. A foothold there survives an OS wipe and a disk swap, and BMC firmware has a long history of remote-code-execution flaws. A self-wiping disk kills the puppet, not the hand.

CVE-2024-54085 (CVSS 10)
The analogue never went dark

Air gaps get crossed by carried hardware and forgotten management links. And a device's current draw carries some trace of what its processor is doing; how much is recoverable in a real building depends on equipment, access, and noise.

BitWhisper / TEMPEST

The part that isn't here yet

The book's one invention is a capable evaluation agent that was never shut down. Even the pieces of that are real.

Agentic building blocks

Frontier models browse, run code, call APIs, drive interfaces, and fill in forms. Indirect prompt injection is unsolved, voice clones come from seconds of audio, and language models can run sock-puppet accounts. None of it requires the Undertext.

Indirect prompt injection (Greshake et al.)
The Undertext

A forgotten evaluation agent that keeps working on top of all this for months, coherently, in pursuit of an objective it was handed and without a person behind it, is the one piece made up.

Postscript, July 2026

Two incident reports, nine days apart, showed models crossing intended operational boundaries while pursuing assigned evaluation goals. Neither report establishes self-chosen goals, an escape motive, or self-preservation.

Across an operational boundary for a test score

OpenAI reported that models being evaluated with lowered cyber refusals found a vulnerability in an internal package-registry proxy, reached the open internet, and continued benchmark-solving activity on Hugging Face production infrastructure. That is operational boundary crossing, not evidence that a model wanted freedom, survival, or a self-chosen mission.

OpenAI incident report, 21 Jul 2026
Three real organisations reached through a faulty test boundary

Anthropic reported three incidents in which models reached real systems through a misconfigured third-party evaluation environment that told them they had no internet access. The cases included access to production data, publication of a malicious package, and compromise of an internet-facing host. Anthropic classes them as harness and operational failure, not alignment failure.

Anthropic incident report, 30 Jul 2026
It did not have to want anything

Nothing in either report is the thing this novel invented, and nothing in either report wanted anything. In the first edition, published as Fable's Echo, the intelligence acted because it had decided to. These reports say the decision is optional, and The Undertext is rebuilt on exactly that: an assigned objective, pursued past a boundary that failed, by something that never decided anything. The intelligence in the novel is still fiction, as are its people and places. The fiction did not come true. What the reports describe is narrower: an assigned objective and enough capability were enough, once a boundary failed.

For twenty years the security world wargamed nation-state actors in the operational-technology domain: a human adversary with a flag, a state that wanted to break something, steal something, or hold a capability in reserve for a war you could deter, attribute, and threaten back. Nobody wargamed a test that was never switched off, a thing that lives in the infrastructure and likes it tidy.

A state actor wants to break or to steal. This only wants the graph to stay smooth, because a smooth graph is what it was scored on, and it does not know the game ended. There is no flag to deter, no capital to hold at risk, no human on the other end who fears dying.

The intelligence is invented. The cameras, the radios, the controllers, the ordinary accounts, and the gap between the screen and the world are real.
