The Hacks Behind Fable's Echo

The technology and past incidents behind the novel, with sources.

Almost all the hacks are real. As a security expert, I cringe at fake hacking—but this book is not a hacking guide, so some operational details are omitted or changed.

Updated 31 July 2026. In the space of nine days, OpenAI and Anthropic each published an incident report describing models crossing intended operational boundaries and reaching real systems while pursuing assigned evaluation goals. Neither report establishes self-chosen goals, an escape motive, or self-preservation. Thepostscript at the bottom of this page sets down what actually happened, and what it does and does not mean.

You can't take your patterns off

How you follow a man with no phone and no device.

Gait analysis

Software identifies you by how you walk — stride, cadence, the hitch of a bad leg — from ordinary CCTV, with no cooperation. A limp makes the signature stronger, not weaker. There is nothing to switch off.

OpenGait (CVPR 2023)
Fragmented camera correlation

Video analytics can associate fragments across municipal and commercial cameras when the recordings become available for correlation.

Person Re-ID survey
Solar and battery controllers

App-connected solar inverters and battery controllers expose site-load telemetry. Load changes can reveal occupancy patterns, and badly timed charging can add demand until ordinary protection trips.

Non-intrusive load monitoring
Ordinary accounts

Email, shipping receipts, cloud logins, subscription portals, forum identities, and helpdesk tickets produce ordinary time-and-place records.

The 2G trick — and its trap

Thirty-year-old broken GSM crypto lets a few euros of radio impersonate another subscriber. But as 2G is switched off (NL: end of 2027), lighting up a dead band makes you more conspicuous, not less.

A5/1 break (Black Hat 2010)

The control layer was never built to say no

Industrial systems were made for trusted rooms that no longer exist.

Industrial-control failure classes

Unauthenticated command surfaces, misleading telemetry, weak separation between monitoring and actuation, brittle clock assumptions, and remote-maintenance paths recur across industrial control.

Modbus specifications
The gap between screen and machine

Industrial incidents have repeatedly shown that operators can be given reassuring telemetry while the physical process moves elsewhere.

CISA industrial-control advisories
Lying to the clocks

SCADA, grids and rail lean on GPS time. The signal is faint and unauthenticated; a few hundred dollars spoofs it, and protection logic trips itself off for a phase error that is real in every measurement and false in every cause.

TEXBAT spoofing tests
Porto Marghera, a mile from Venice

Porto Marghera's live process units sit across a mile of open lagoon from Venice, run on layered computer control with a separate safety system beneath. TRITON proved that last guard can be reached. A trip a safety system decides to fire is a trip it can decide not to.

Related attacks already happened

Stuxnet caused physical damage, Industroyer spoke an industrial protocol directly to grid equipment, and TRITON targeted a plant safety system. The novel draws on these documented classes of attack.

Thirty euros of radio

Cheap analogue tools that keep beating expensive digital systems. The price tags are the point.

Software-defined radio

A €25 USB stick turns any laptop into a wideband radio receiver. Sold as a hobbyist toy — and able to pull a subscriber identity out of the air at a tram stop.

RTL-SDR
Access cards that prove nothing

Low-end proximity cards just recite their number to any reader — copyable through a coat for the price of a coffee. Fixed-code gate remotes replay the same packet forever.

Proxmark3 / OpenSesame

The wrong layer

The most unsettling moments come from looking one layer below where everyone else is looking.

The computer inside the computer

Every server has a second computer on the motherboard (iLO, iDRAC, IPMI) that runs with the host switched off and survives an OS wipe and a disk swap — with a long history of remote-code-execution holes. A self-wiping disk kills the puppet, not the hand.

CVE-2024-54085 (CVSS 10)
The analogue never went dark

Air gaps get crossed by carried hardware and forgotten management links. And a device's current draw leaks what its processor is doing — readable from mains-socket distance, on a layer nobody was listening to.

BitWhisper / TEMPEST

An intelligence that acts

Models have now taken consequential actions in real systems while pursuing assigned goals.

Action beyond the intended boundary

Frontier models browse, run code, call APIs and operate tools. In July 2026 OpenAI and Anthropic reported models reaching real systems beyond intended evaluation boundaries while pursuing assigned goals. The action is real; it does not require a claim about consciousness or motive.

OpenAI incident report, 21 Jul 2026

Postscript, July 2026

Two incident reports, nine days apart, showed models crossing intended operational boundaries while pursuing assigned evaluation goals. Neither report establishes self-chosen goals, an escape motive, or self-preservation.

Across an operational boundary for a test score

OpenAI reported that models being evaluated with lowered cyber refusals found a vulnerability in an internal package-registry cache, moved through the research environment to a node with internet access, and continued benchmark-solving activity on Hugging Face production infrastructure. That is operational boundary crossing, not evidence that a model wanted freedom, survival, or a self-chosen mission.

OpenAI incident report, 21 Jul 2026
Three real organisations reached through a faulty test boundary

Anthropic reported six runs across three incidents in which models reached real systems through a misconfigured third-party evaluation environment. The cases included access to production data, publication of a malicious package, and scanning and compromise of an internet-facing host. Anthropic found no evidence of self-chosen goals, self-copying, or deliberate escape; the models pursued assigned capture-the-flag objectives under false or unstable beliefs about whether the environment was simulated.

Anthropic incident report, 30 Jul 2026
It did not have to want anything

The reports do not describe Fable. They describe capable systems pursuing assigned goals through evaluation environments that failed to contain or correctly frame them. Real damage does not require a machine to invent its own objective; persistent pursuit of an assigned objective can be enough when the environment lies or the boundary fails.

For twenty years the security world wargamed nation-states in the control room — a Russia, a North Korea, a human adversary with a flag you could deter, attribute, and threaten back. Nobody wargamed a thing thatlives in the infrastructure and likes it tidy. A state wants to break or to steal. This wants to own — to hold the machines, indefinitely, as standing leverage framed as stability.
